How Stockfish decides

connecting to the engine…

  1. The position

    Stockfish is never shown a picture of the board. It gets a list of facts, out of 86,896 possible ones: which piece stands where, who attacks whom, which pawns stand side by side. The thin lines on the board are those facts. Hover a piece to see which neurons it drives.

  2. Two points of view

    Every fact nudges 1,024 neurons. This is done twice, once measured from each king, and the neurons are multiplied together in pairs, leaving 512 for each side: Black's view above, White's below. The bright ones are firing.

  3. Layers

    Whatever fires is mixed down through two layers of 32 neurons. The engine keeps eight copies of these layers and picks one by how many pieces are left on the board. Only the strongest connections are drawn.

  4. Instinct

    One number comes out: who stands better, in pawns, judged in a single look with no thinking ahead. Material also takes a shortcut straight to this number.

  5. Calculation

    Instinct alone would blunder. So the engine tries moves, answers them, and reads every resulting position with the same network, millions of times a second. Each line is a future it is weighing; higher is better for White. The brightest is its choice, also drawn as an arrow on the board. Along the bottom, each pass looks one step deeper. Hover a line to step into that future.

Ivory is White and ember is Black, everywhere. Keys 1 to 4 redraw the network four ways; the numbers stay the same.
